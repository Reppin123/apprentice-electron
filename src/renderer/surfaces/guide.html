Apprentice · how it works

Everything it does.
Every key that wakes it.

Apprentice is an assistant that lives on your Mac. You talk to it out loud, it can see and act on whatever's on your screen, and it learns how you like to work. This page shows you all of it — and you can try things as you read: press the real keys, click into the panels. Nothing here is a screenshot; it all actually works.

You talk, it listens It sees your screen It does tasks for you It learns how you work
01 · the basics

Talk to it like a person.

There's no window to open and no chat box to find. You reach Apprentice by holding a key and speaking — in any app, anywhere on your Mac. Ask for something and it gets to work, usually while you carry on with whatever you were doing. It can even handle a few jobs at once in the background.

Two words you'll see throughout this guide: a job running in the background is called a strand — you can have several at once. And Summon is the single panel where you see and control everything (you pop it open with a keyboard shortcut). Both are explained just ahead.

02 · what it can do

Not sure what to ask? Start here.

Apprentice isn't a chat box you copy answers out of — it can actually do things on your Mac. This is the range. Each line is a real thing you could just say out loud; whatever you're working in, there's probably a way to hand part of it over.

However you'd say it to a person, you can say it to Apprentice. And when you're not sure, just ask it “what can you do?”

03 · the keyring

Six keys do almost everything.

Modifiers, the Mac way: ⌃ control · ⌥ option · ⌘ command. Hold a real combo on your keyboard and watch its line light up below.

Start with ⌃ + ⌥ — hold them together and the talk line should glow.
04 · the main panel · ⌘⌥S

One panel for everything.

Press ⌘⌥S and a panel slides up from the bottom of the screen. This is Summon — your home base. There's a box at the bottom you can type or talk into, and whatever you ask for appears above it. No menus to dig through; you just say where you want to go. It opens on your running jobs, and from there you can open any one of them, revisit rested ones (embers), change settings, or see which apps it's connected to. It's live below — type in the box, or click the words under it.

Click to look around: your jobs · open one · embers · settings · connected apps. Each job is a colored dot: a dot that's gently glowing is working right now, and the amber one is waiting on an answer from you. Click a dot to open that job.

05 · what it knows · ⌘⌥J

See what it knows about you.

Everything Apprentice picks up lives in one window, the Inspector. Rules are the habits it's learned — how you write, format, and name things — and the brighter a rule looks, the surer it is. Memory is facts it holds about you, your people, and your projects. Turns is a log of everything you've asked and how it handled it. Noticed is its own daily self-review — what it'd fix about itself. And it keeps your saved workflows plus a live usage meter for cost and speed. Click any tab — it's the real window.

06 · how a rule is born

It watches, then it's sure.

Apprentice doesn't come pre-loaded with your preferences — it notices them. The first time it spots a pattern in your work, it writes down a faint, unsure rule. When it sees the same thing again — another file, another day — that rule gets more certain (and in the Inspector, brighter). Every rule remembers where it came from: something it noticed in your work, something you told it directly, or a starting habit it came with.

07 · learn from your work

Point it at your past work.

You don't have to wait for it to learn you one request at a time. Say “learn how I write from my sent emails” (or point it at any folder of your files) and it reads through them in four steps, then saves what it found as new rules. You'll see the progress as it goes, and you can review everything it picked up.

08 · correct it once

Say it once. It sticks.

When you correct it mid-work — out loud or typed — it writes that down immediately as a rule marked you taught this, ranked above anything it merely inferred. If it held an older, conflicting rule, that one is retired — struck through in the mind, kept on record so you can always see what changed.

09 · workflows

It keeps the recipe — when you ask.

Some jobs take several steps to work out. When Apprentice finishes one, it can save the whole thing as a small program it can run again — but only if you want it to. It never stockpiles these on its own: it offers to save, or saves when you tell it to. After that, asking for the same thing just runs the saved recipe instead of working it out again. They all show up in the Inspector's workflows tab — with a name, when to use it, and when it last worked.

the short version

You hold a key and speak. It gets to work, learns every time you correct it, and remembers how next time.

Everything else is just where to look.

Two windows do most of it — Summon (⌘⌥S) to run things, the Inspector (⌘⌥J) to see what it knows. A handful of keys for the rest.
